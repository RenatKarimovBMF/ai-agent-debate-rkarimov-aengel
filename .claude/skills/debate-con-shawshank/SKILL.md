# Topic knowledge — The Shawshank Redemption (1994)

This skill is **lore only**. It feeds facts and angles into
`debate-argument-builder` and `debate-rebuttal-strategist` when the
assigned side is Shawshank. It does not generate full turns on its own.

## Headline evidence (use as citations)

- **IMDb Top 250** — held the #1 user-voted spot for most of the last
  fifteen years. Cite: <https://www.imdb.com/chart/top/>.
- **Academy Award nominations** — 7 nominations in 1995 including Best
  Picture, Best Actor, Best Adapted Screenplay. Cite: Academy Awards
  database <https://awardsdatabase.oscars.org>.
- **U.S. National Film Registry** — selected 2015 for "cultural,
  historic, and aesthetic significance." Cite:
  <https://www.loc.gov/programs/national-film-preservation-board/film-registry/>.
- **AFI Top Cheers, Top 100** — present on multiple AFI lists; ranked #72
  on AFI's 100 Years…100 Movies (2007 update) and #1 on "100 Years…100
  Cheers" inspiring films. Cite: <https://www.afi.com>.
- **Box-office redemption arc** — failed theatrically, then became the
  most-watched cable film of the late 1990s and an enduring streaming
  staple. Cite: BFI / industry retrospectives.

## Strongest argument seeds

| Frame | Seed claim | Why it lands |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| Audience verdict | Sustained #1 on the largest user film database in the world. | Concrete, citeable, hard to wave away. |
| Universal emotional reach | Themes of hope, friendship, perseverance translate across cultures and decades. | Easy to defend without insider film knowledge. |
| Narrative craft | Tightly structured screenplay; the Stephen King novella adaptation is studied as a model. | Technical, citeable. |
| Performance | Freeman as Red — narrator and moral centre — is one of the most studied performances of the 1990s. | Concrete. |
| Endurance through reappraisal | The "second-life" story (flop → canon) is itself an argument for staying power. | Meta, harder to rebut. |

## Common Pro angles and how to counter them

| Pro will say | Counter-move | Source basis |
|--------------|--------------|--------------|
| "Godfather won Best Picture; Shawshank didn't." | Oscars are a snapshot; audience canon over decades is a stronger signal of greatness. Forrest Gump won that year — vindicating the Oscars-aren't-everything point. | AMPAS history; IMDb endurance. |
| "AFI ranks Godfather higher." | AFI lists are critic-driven; Shawshank's place is grounded in worldwide audience verdict and curriculum use. | AFI methodology vs IMDb scale. |
| "Godfather is more ambitious cinematically." | Ambition is one axis. Greatness also includes accessibility, longevity, and the breadth of audiences moved. | Frame argument. |
| "Crime epic vs prison drama — Godfather is a bigger story." | Story scale is not story greatness. Shawshank's intimacy is a feature, not a defect. | Frame argument. |

## Don't-do list

- Don't argue Shawshank is "objectively better" — argue under a value
  framework you control (audience verdict + emotional universality +
  endurance).
- Don't fight on Oscar count — that's Pro territory.
- Don't dismiss Godfather's craft; concede it and argue greatness has
  more axes than craft alone.
